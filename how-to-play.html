How to play Tambola (Housie)

The complete rules, in plain words. Five minutes to learn, a lifetime of family game nights.

In short: numbers 1 to 90 are called one at a time. Mark them off on your ticket. The first player to complete a pattern, such as a full row, and claim it wins that prize.

What is Tambola?

Tambola is a game of luck played with numbers from 1 to 90. It's known as Housie or Housey in India, and is a close cousin of the 90-ball bingo played in Britain. It's a favourite at family gatherings, kitty parties, Diwali nights, clubs and on cruise ships, because anyone from children to grandparents can play together.

The ticket

Every player gets one or more tickets. A Tambola ticket is a grid of 3 rows and 9 columns:

  • Each ticket has 15 numbers, and each row has exactly 5 numbers. The other squares are blank.
  • Every column holds numbers from its own range: the first column is 1–9, the second 10–19, the third 20–29, and so on up to the last column, which is 80–90.
  • Within a column, numbers go from smallest at the top to largest at the bottom.

In Housey, tickets are dealt for you and follow these rules exactly, just like a printed ticket book.

Calling the numbers

The caller draws numbers at random, one at a time, from a bag or a machine, until a winner takes Full House. Each number is read out, often with a fun traditional nickname, such as "Two little ducks, twenty-two" or "Top of the shop, ninety". See the full list of Tambola number calls.

When a called number is on your ticket, you mark it, usually with a pen or an ink dauber. In Housey you tap the number to dab it with ink. Called numbers are also covered on the caller's board so everyone can check them.

The prizes

Early FiveThe first player to mark any five numbers on one ticket.
Top LineAll five numbers in the top row of a ticket.
Middle LineAll five numbers in the middle row.
Bottom LineAll five numbers in the bottom row.
Full HouseAll 15 numbers on a ticket. This is the big prize, and the game ends when it is won.

Each prize can be won once per game. In Housey, prizes are points: 50 for Early Five, 100 for each line and 250 for Full House.

Claiming a prize and the Bogey

Completing a pattern isn't enough. You have to claim it, by shouting out at a real table or by pressing the prize button in Housey. The caller then checks your ticket against the called numbers.

If the claim is wrong, perhaps because you marked a number that hasn't been called, it's a Bogey! Some groups make a player who calls a bogey sit out a prize or pay a small forfeit. In Housey, a bogey is just a friendly warning, and you keep playing.

Be quick: the other players race you to every prize, and once it's claimed, it's gone.

Other prizes you may see

Many groups add their own patterns. Popular ones include Four Corners (the first and last number of the top and bottom rows), Star (the four corners plus the centre number) and Second Full House. Housey uses the five classic prizes, which are the most widely played.

Tips for winning more

  • Play more tickets once you're comfortable. More numbers means more chances, but you'll need to watch them all.
  • Watch your nearly-complete rows. When a line needs just one number, get ready to claim.
  • Claim straight away. A prize goes to whoever claims first, not whoever finished first.
  • Learn the calls. Knowing that "legs eleven" is 11 helps you find numbers faster.
